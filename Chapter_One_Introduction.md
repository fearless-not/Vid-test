# [Heading 1] Chapter One: Introduction

## [Heading 2] 1.1 Background to the Student Industrial Work Experience Scheme

### [Heading 3] 1.1.1 Purpose of the industrial attachment

The Student Industrial Work Experience Scheme provided the practical setting through which I connected architectural education with professional work. As a student of the Department of Architecture, Covenant University, I had previously encountered architectural design, representation, and digital production within an academic environment. The industrial attachment placed these areas within the context of an active creative practice, where work was undertaken in response to instructions, project requirements, reviews, site conditions, and presentation needs.

I completed my industrial attachment at Moe + Art Architecture between 2 March 2026 and 31 August 2026. The placement lasted 26 weeks, as recorded in the placement documentation. I was attached to the Design Team, although I also interacted with personnel involved in site activities, installation, fabrication, administration, accounts, and the AWCA team. This exposure enabled me to observe that architectural practice extended beyond the preparation of drawings. It also involved communication, design development, model production, visualisation, documentation, site observation, material consideration, coordination, and response to feedback.

The training was important because it placed my academic knowledge within a professional workflow. In school, I had worked on design assignments under academic studio conditions. During the attachment, I had to interpret instructions, work within existing project files, respond to corrections, meet task expectations, communicate with other people, and understand how design decisions related to actual sites and project activities. The experience therefore gave practical meaning to the connection between architectural education and professional responsibility.

### [Heading 3] 1.1.2 Relevance of the scheme to architectural education

The attachment was relevant to my architectural education because the work involved several areas that formed part of architectural training. These included design development, technical drawing, computer-aided drafting, three-dimensional modelling, real-time visualisation, site and context documentation, interior and spatial consideration, exhibition installation, and project coordination. The work also required me to observe how digital models, drawings, photographs, site information, and physical conditions supported the development and communication of architectural ideas.

The placement also introduced a level of professional interaction that could not be fully reproduced in the classroom. I participated in site meetings, daily work assignments, presentations, internal reviews, and discussions with other people working on projects. I learned that architectural communication involved both the clarity of the drawing or model and the ability to explain, receive, and apply feedback. This was especially relevant to my expectation of learning how to speak to clients and interact with fellow architects.

The official SIWES materials supplied for this report also framed industrial training as a means of connecting academic preparation with practical experience. I therefore treated the attachment as an opportunity to examine how the skills acquired in the university were applied, adjusted, and extended within a professional architectural environment.

### [Heading 3] 1.2.2 How I obtained and selected the placement

I obtained the placement through a personal application and a recommendation. The selection process included a portfolio review. I chose Moe + Art Architecture because I considered it one of the best firms available to me and was attracted by the quality of its works and designs. I was also encouraged by the manner in which the firm responded during the placement process. These factors influenced my decision to pursue the attachment there rather than treating the placement as only an administrative requirement.

The placement began after my portfolio had been reviewed. The process introduced me to an early professional expectation: an applicant’s previous work and ability to present ideas could influence access to an architectural workplace. This made the portfolio more than a collection of school exercises. It became a means of communicating my interests, abilities, and readiness to learn.

### [Heading 3] 1.2.3 Expectations before the attachment

Before commencing the internship, I expected to learn a wide range of software and improve my understanding of rendering. I also expected to learn how to speak to clients and interact with fellow architects. These expectations covered both technical and interpersonal development. I wanted to improve my ability to produce architectural work digitally, but I also wanted to understand how architects communicated within a team and with people outside the design team.

My expectations were relevant to the activities eventually recorded in the logbook. The attachment involved Rhino 7, Revit 2023, AutoCAD, and Twinmotion. It also exposed me to site meetings, project discussions, presentation activities, and feedback. I therefore encountered both the digital and human aspects of the professional practice that I had expected to study.

### [Heading 3] 1.2.4 Main objectives of the training

The objectives of my training were understood through the official logbook, the placement documentation, and the activities carried out during the 26-week period. They included the following:

1. To gain practical experience in architectural design and design development.
2. To improve my ability to produce and understand technical drawings.
3. To develop competence in Rhino 7, Revit 2023, AutoCAD, and Twinmotion.
4. To understand how models, drawings, maps, renders, and site photographs supported project development.
5. To observe site conditions and participate in site-related activities.
6. To improve my communication with architects, clients, supervisors, and other project participants.

8. To develop professional discipline, teamwork, and the ability to respond to feedback.

These objectives were not treated as separate from the daily work. They were pursued through design assignments, modelling exercises, site meetings, exhibition and installation activities, site documentation, reviews, and interactions with members of the company.

### [Heading 3] 1.3.2 History and development

Moe + Art Architecture was established as a unified practice in 2014 and was led by Creative Director Papa Omotayo. The company history supplied for this report described the practice as a collaborative creative studio based in Lagos, Nigeria, working at the intersection of architecture, urban design, and artistic production. It further described the practice as multidisciplinary, community-driven, and ecologically sensitive in its approach to contemporary African spaces.

The supplied history stated that Moe + Art Architecture developed through a strategic merger between MOE Limited and O+O Architects, with O+O Architects described as a Lagos- and London-based architecture firm. The merger brought together more than 20 years of individual experience in strategic, innovative design and value-driven delivery. Following its establishment as a unified entity in 2014, the practice developed into a studio involving urbanists, architects, artists, interior designers, and product developers.

The information supplied also described cross-collaboration between Moe + Art Architecture and its sister agency, AWCA. This relationship was presented as a means of combining spatial design with wider questions of design, branding, programming, and long-term social engagement. I encountered evidence of this wider creative relationship through the company identification material and through the mixture of architectural, artistic, installation, and spatial activities represented in the project evidence.

The company history and development information used in this section was supplied with external source links for later inclusion in the IEEE References section. Those sources will be listed fully when the complete report is assembled.
